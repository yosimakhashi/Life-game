    Vakku Urappikkal

    The elders of both families meet and give their word: you both agree and the marriage is confirmed. Plan the visit here.

    To invite someone, add them in the Guests tab and tick Vakku Urappikkal.

    What to do

    • Have the house, seating and food ready at least an hour before they arrive.
    • Greet the elders first and welcome everyone warmly at the door.
    • Serve water or tea soon after guests sit down.
    • Let the elders lead the talk. Your family's chosen elder speaks for your side.
    • Listen more than you speak, and stay calm and respectful.
    • Dress neatly and modestly. The whole family should look well put together.
    • Give one person charge of the food and another charge of looking after guests.
    • Write down everything that is agreed: dates, next steps, who does what.
    • Thank every guest before they leave, and see the elders off to their vehicles.
    • Call the relatives you invited afterwards and thank them for coming.

    What not to do

    • Don't ask for or discuss dowry. It hurts relations, and in India it is illegal.
    • Don't argue or bargain in front of guests. Talk about disagreements privately later.
    • Don't raise old family issues or sensitive topics.
    • Don't promise dates, expenses or arrangements your family hasn't agreed on yet.
    • Don't invite too many people. Keep it to close family and respected elders.
    • Don't overspend on food or decoration. Save for the engagement and wedding.
    • Don't stay on your phone while the elders are talking.
    • Don't leave guests sitting alone or waiting without food and drinks.
    • Don't post photos online until both families are happy with it.

    Things to consider

    • How many people: how many are coming from their side, and whether your space fits them plus your own guests.
    • Time of visit: a morning or evening visit needs tea and snacks, while lunch or dinner time needs a full meal.
    • Elderly guests: comfortable chairs, and sugar-free or light food options for those with health needs.
    • Prayer time: if the visit overlaps with prayer, keep a clean space ready.
    • Gifts: check with your family beforehand whether sweets, fruits or gifts are exchanged.
    • Weather and parking: keep a plan for rain and space for vehicles.
    • Budget: decide in advance how much to spend on this event.
    • You two: Asif and Alfiya should agree between yourselves first on what you both want.

    Topics that may come up

    • Date for the engagement
    • Rough time frame or date for the nikah / wedding
    • Where the wedding will be held and roughly how many guests
    • Customs and traditions each family follows
    • Mahr and other arrangements, discussed respectfully by the elders
    • Who will take care of which arrangements
    • The next meeting or visit

    Talk these over in your family before the visit, so everyone gives the same answer.
